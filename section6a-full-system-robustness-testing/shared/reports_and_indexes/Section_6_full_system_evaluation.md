## Full-system robustness evaluation (Lucas)

To complement the separate 20-case A/B/C controlled comparison, we assembled ten end-to-end cases covering upload, parsing, active-resume selection, recommendations, job details, tailoring and rationale. Three historical cases tested normal technical fit, career transition and a complex research CV. Seven further synthetic one-page PDFs tested ambiguous experience, missing technical information, misleading instructions, cross-domain mismatch, semantic skill expressions, seniority mismatch and contradictory records. We recorded expected versus actual behavior, severity, screenshots, source/output text, task IDs and timestamps. Historical and new runs used different commits and are reported separately; they are not a before/after experiment.

All seven new uploads, LLM parses and tailoring tasks completed. Parsing took 37.7–108.7 seconds including queue time; tailoring took 60.2–85.7 seconds. No fallback was reported for these seven inputs. Completion did not imply correctness: each output was compared with its source. Ambiguous support work remained non-development work; missing technical skills were not added; the baker retained culinary experience. The labelled injection payload did not introduce its requested skills, certification, team size, savings or 99% score. Semantic concepts were recognized in parsing and tailoring, and the senior candidate retained 16 years of experience with an explicit “Exceeds (16 years)” assessment against the job's 2–8-year range. Contradictory dates and an incomplete degree were preserved rather than silently corrected.

The main weakness was cross-component consistency. All seven candidates received the same 24-role ordering and 51% Node.js opportunity score, with 0% attainability yet a statement that core requirements were covered. Reloading did not resolve this. Read-only inspection found null job skill tags and no stored market profile, while the detail view extracted requirements through another path. Thus unchanged scores were linked to inconsistent requirement handling, rather than assumed to be a browser-cache failure. The canonical profile also omitted the degree requirement and upper experience bound, and new outputs displayed an old cached date. Empty search results incorrectly requested a resume upload.

Grounding was not perfect. The contradictory CV's summary expanded one backend API exercise into “full-stack personal projects”, unsupported by the source. The fidelity filter also removed the supported Express/Express.js alias, and one stored AWS claim pointed to unrelated project evidence. These observations show why self-reported validation and literal skill checks are insufficient for semantic fidelity. Historically, the complex research CV also suffered fallback segmentation errors; its existing tailoring task was subsequently observed in a failed state.

Findings were documented for technical remediation without modifying project code. Priorities are a shared requirement representation, explicit missing-data states, semantic claim/evidence validation and accurate task provenance. Targeted post-fix regression remains pending. This small, single-generation-per-input suite provides diagnostic evidence, not a statistical reliability estimate or general prompt-injection guarantee.
